The Reality

Your agent is busy. Do you know what it's doing?

You set up an AI agent to save time—drafting emails, scheduling calendar invites, and accessing tools.

But right now, your agent has your API keys, credentials, and access tokens sitting directly inside its environment. If anything goes wrong or a plugin gets hacked, they are exposed.

And when your agent takes an action, it just does it. The approve button in your chat app is a convenience, not a secure checkpoint.

You deserve better than hoping nothing goes wrong.

The AC2 Standard

What changes when you install AC2

Safety shouldn't mean friction. AC2 makes agent protection intuitive, immediate, and zero-compromise.

Your credentials stay on your device

Your API keys, tokens, and passwords never leave your phone. Your agent gets permission to act — but it never gets the actual keys. If something goes wrong with the agent, your credentials are safe.

Nothing happens without your say-so

Before your agent takes any sensitive action, you see exactly what it wants to do on your device. You approve with a tap and your fingerprint. No approval, no action. It's that simple.

Five minutes to set up. Then it's invisible.

AC2 works in the background. You install it, connect your agent, and forget about it — until your phone buzzes with something that needs your attention. No new workflows. No complex dashboards.

Under the hood, AC2 uses WebAuthn passkeys and a direct peer-to-peer connection — your phone and your agent talk directly, with no server in the middle holding your secrets. But you don't need to know any of that. It just works.
Credential Sovereignty

Your keys never leave your hands

Giving an agent raw credentials is like giving someone a copy of your house key. If their servers are breached or a plugin goes rogue, your access is completely compromised.

AC2 flips this. Your credentials stay locked on your device. When your agent needs to act, it asks your phone. You authorize that specific action and your device signs it—the agent never gets the key itself.

It's the difference between handing over your house key and buzzing a guest through the intercom.
